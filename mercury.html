Mercury's Info

Mercury is the smallest and innermost planet of the Solar System, orbiting the Sun at an average distance of about 58 million kilometers. Due to its close proximity to the Sun, Mercury completes one revolution in just 88 Earth days, making it the fastest-orbiting planet. Despite being so close, it is not the hottest planet—Venus holds that title—because Mercury lacks a thick atmosphere to trap heat. Its surface is heavily cratered, resembling Earth’s Moon, and bears evidence of intense meteor impacts over billions of years. Mercury has a large iron core relative to its size, which generates a weak magnetic field, an unusual feature for such a small planet. The planet has no moons and no rings, emphasizing its simplicity compared to the gas giants.
One of Mercury’s most extreme characteristics is its dramatic temperature variation, ranging from about 430°C during the day to −180°C at night. This occurs because Mercury’s atmosphere is extremely thin, composed mainly of oxygen, sodium, and hydrogen, offering almost no insulation. A single Mercury day (sunrise to sunrise) lasts 176 Earth days, meaning the Sun rises very slowly in its sky. Interestingly, water ice has been discovered in permanently shadowed craters near Mercury’s poles, where sunlight never reaches. These discoveries have significantly changed our understanding of the planet. Studying Mercury helps scientists learn more about planetary formation, core dynamics, and how planets behave under extreme solar conditions, making it a key object of interest in planetary science.

  • Mercury has no moons or rings.

  • Temperatures on Mercury range from −180°C at night to 430°C in daytime.
  • Mercury’s atmosphere is extremely thin and is called an exosphere.
  • Water ice exists at its poles, hidden in permanently shadowed craters.
  • From Mercury, the Sun appears about three times larger than it does from Earth.
  • The planet shrinks slowly as its core cools, creating giant surface cliffs.
