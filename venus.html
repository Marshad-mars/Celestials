Venus's Info

Venus is the second planet from the Sun and is often called Earth’s “twin” because of its similar size, mass, and composition, yet it is one of the most hostile worlds in the Solar System. It has a thick atmosphere dominated by carbon dioxide, with clouds of sulfuric acid that completely cover the planet and prevent direct observation of its surface from space. This dense atmosphere causes an extreme greenhouse effect, trapping heat and raising surface temperatures to about 465°C, hot enough to melt lead. Venus rotates very slowly on its axis and unusually spins in a retrograde direction, meaning the Sun appears to rise in the west and set in the east. A single day on Venus is longer than its year.
The surface of Venus is shaped largely by volcanic and tectonic activity rather than plate tectonics like Earth’s. Vast lava plains, large shield volcanoes, and circular features called coronae dominate its landscape, indicating intense geological processes in the past. Impact craters on Venus are relatively few and evenly distributed, suggesting that the planet underwent a massive resurfacing event hundreds of millions of years ago. Despite its proximity to Earth, Venus lacks liquid water today, though scientists believe it may once have had oceans before runaway greenhouse conditions took over. Venus is also the brightest planet in the night sky, appearing as the Morning Star or Evening Star, and has played a significant role in astronomy, mythology, and early studies of planetary atmospheres.
These are some of the following 13 interesting factual points about the "planet_venus"
  • Venus is the hottest planet in the Solar System, even hotter than Mercury, despite being farther from the Sun.
  • A day on Venus (one full rotation) is longer than a year on Venus (one full orbit around the Sun).
  • Venus rotates in a retrograde direction, meaning it spins opposite to the direction of most planets.
  • The atmosphere of Venus is about 96% carbon dioxide, creating an extreme greenhouse effect.
  • Surface temperatures on Venus remain nearly constant day and night, averaging around 465°C.
  • Venus has clouds made of sulfuric acid, which reflect sunlight and make the planet very bright.
  • It is the brightest natural object in the night sky after the Moon.
  • Venus has no moons or ring system.
  • The atmospheric pressure on Venus is about 90 times that of Earth, similar to being deep under Earth’s oceans.
  • Most of Venus’s surface is covered by vast volcanic plains formed by ancient lava flows.
  • Venus shows phases like the Moon when observed from Earth, supporting the heliocentric model.
