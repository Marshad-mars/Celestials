Saturn's Info

Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest planet in the Solar System, famous for its magnificent ring system. It is a gas giant primarily composed of hydrogen and helium, with no solid surface like Earth. Saturn has a very low average density, so low that it could theoretically float in water if a body large enough existed. The planet rotates rapidly, completing one rotation in about ten and a half hours, which causes it to bulge at the equator. Saturn’s pale yellow appearance is due to ammonia crystals in its upper atmosphere, along with subtle cloud bands and powerful winds.
One of Saturn’s most remarkable features is its extensive ring system, made of billions of particles of ice, rock, and dust ranging in size from tiny grains to large boulders. These rings are divided into several main sections, each separated by gaps such as the Cassini Division. Saturn also has a large number of moons, over 140 confirmed, including Titan, the second-largest moon in the Solar System. Titan is especially important because it has a thick atmosphere and liquid methane lakes. Studying Saturn helps scientists understand gas giants, planetary formation, and the early history of the Solar System.
